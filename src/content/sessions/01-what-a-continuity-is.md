## The technique

A continuity is every fact a story has told its audience and cannot take back.
It is not the plot. It is the record the plot leaves behind: who is dead, what
the rules are, what happened in which year. The audience keeps that record
whether the studio does or not.

A continuity fails in one of three ways:

- a **contradiction**: two established facts that cannot both be true
- an **orphan**: a setup the story never paid off
- **drift**: a character or a rule that changes with no cause in the story

## The case: the Mandarin

In _Iron Man_ (2008), Tony Stark is held captive by a terrorist group called the
Ten Rings. In _Iron Man 3_ (2013), a terrorist called the Mandarin broadcasts
under the Ten Rings' symbol, and turns out to be Trevor Slattery, an actor hired
by Aldrich Killian. The twist works on its own terms. It also leaves the real
Ten Rings, from the first film, with nobody in charge and no explanation.

The One-Shot _All Hail the King_ (2014) says a real Mandarin exists, and did not
enjoy the impression. The films then say nothing for seven years, until
_Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings_ (2021) introduces Wenwu, and
Trevor, still alive.

## In the seminar

We audit the Mandarin together, the way the Continuity Audit asks: locate both
sides, classify the failure, and argue for the class. Is it a contradiction or
an orphan? Most rooms split. Then we list what it would take to trace it to a
production decision, and who would have had to say so in public.
