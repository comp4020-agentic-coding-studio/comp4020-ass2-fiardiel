The course asks one question: what does a long-running story owe the people who
remember it? Before it can be answered, you need to be able to prove that a
story broke a promise. This lecture is how.

## Outline

- **The record.** What counts as established, and why the audience keeps it
  whether the studio does or not.
- **Locate.** Both sides of a failure, each cited to a title and a scene.
- **Classify.** Contradiction, orphan or drift, and how to argue between the two
  that fit best.
- **Trace.** From the failure back to the decision behind it. What counts as a
  public source: an interview, a trade report, a release schedule, a casting
  announcement. What doesn't: a fan theory, however good.

The method is the Continuity Audit's structure, in the same order. The Mandarin,
in tomorrow's seminar, is the worked example.
